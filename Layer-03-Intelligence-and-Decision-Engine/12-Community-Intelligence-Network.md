# Community Intelligence Network

## Purpose
The community layer captures structured local knowledge that external ratings often miss. It is designed around useful evidence rather than engagement volume.

## Contribution Types
- Attribute confirmation.
- Factual correction.
- Experience report.
- Question and answer.
- Service availability confirmation.
- Accessibility information.
- Structured recommendation context.
- Duplicate/closure report.
- Location correction.

## Contributor Quality
A contributor reputation signal may consider:
- Historical accuracy.
- Corroboration.
- Quality/completeness.
- Consistency.
- Successful corrections.
- Moderation history.
- Abuse indicators.

Reputation should not create an untouchable class of users. Every contribution remains subject to evidence and moderation.

## Validation
Community facts may be:
- Unverified.
- Community-supported.
- Corroborated by independent sources.
- Business-confirmed.
- PIOS-verified through approved evidence.

The interface should not collapse these states into a single “verified” badge.

## Moderation
Moderation covers:
- Spam.
- Coordinated manipulation.
- Harassment.
- Hate/abuse.
- Fraudulent claims.
- PII exposure.
- Unsafe content.
- Promotional misuse.
- Duplicate content.

## Business Corrections
Businesses can dispute or correct factual claims through a documented process. They cannot delete legitimate criticism solely because it is unfavorable.

## Incentive Design
Reward useful accuracy, not posting frequency. Avoid mechanics that encourage low-quality contribution farming.

## Auditability
Moderation decisions, escalations, business disputes, and high-impact contributor restrictions require reason codes and audit records.
